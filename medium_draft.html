You have 8 × A100s (80 GB each). That's 640 GB of GPU memory. Your 7B model needs 98 GB. Simple math says it should fit six times over.

So why does training crash with an OOM error?

Because 98 GB is just the parameters. By the time you add gradients and Adam optimizer states, every GPU needs 98 GB per replica — and DDP puts a full replica on every single GPU. Your 640 GB cluster holds exactly one copy of the model, with nothing left for activations or batch data.

This is the memory trap that stops most teams from scaling past 7B parameters.

ZeRO — sharding the redundancy away

The ZeRO paper asked a simple question: why does every GPU need a full copy of everything? It doesn't. ZeRO removes the redundancy in three stages:

ZeRO-1 shards optimizer states. Memory halves — but adds 50% more communication than DDP. Rarely the best choice.

ZeRO-2 shards optimizer states and gradients. Memory drops to ~3.5 B/param. And here's the surprising part: it adds zero communication overhead compared to DDP. Same 2× |model| per step, just rearranged. This is almost always the right first move when DDP doesn't fit.

ZeRO-3 / FSDP shards everything — including the parameters. Memory scales as 14/N B/param. A 70B model on 8 GPUs needs ~24.5 GB instead of 980 GB. The cost: 50% more communication than DDP.

The insight most tutorials miss

ZeRO-3 forces FSDP to reconstruct each layer's weights on the fly. The backward pass computes two distinct gradients per layer:

  • ∂ℒ/∂x — the activation gradient, passed backward through the chain rule. Stays full on every GPU. No communication.
  • ∂ℒ/∂W — the weight gradient. Immediately Reduce-Scattered and sharded. No GPU ever holds the full weight gradient.
